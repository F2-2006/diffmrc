\chapter{Resolution of identity (RI/DF)}
\label{ch:ri-df}

\section{Purpose and scope}

The four-center two-electron integrals of the multireference chain are
the memory bottleneck of its nuclear-derivative path.  Under
\texttt{pyscfad}, the derivative of \texttt{int2e} allocates a dense
\texttt{nctr}~$\times$~\texttt{nao}$^4$ array before any chemistry
happens: 55.0~GB at \texttt{nao}~$=96$ and 2033~GB at \texttt{nao}~$=175$
for the systems measured here.  The module \texttt{diffmrc.core.ri}
replaces the four-center tensor in the correlation stage by two
three-center factors and a metric, and supplies a nuclear-derivative
path whose working set is \texttt{nao}$^2$~$\times$~\texttt{naux} per
atom.

The layer applies to any energy assembled from two-electron integrals
in a Gaussian basis, provided the caller supplies a matching auxiliary
basis and accepts the density-fitting approximation.  It does not
make the reference SCF layer RI; the SCF layer remains dense unless the
separate RIJONX driver of Chapter~\ref{ch:new-modules} is used.  It
does not provide analytic second derivatives, and it does not provide
an actinide fitting basis beyond the elements covered by the def2 and
cc-pVDZ-RI families.  Those boundaries are stated again in
Section~\ref{sec:ri-pitfalls}.

\section{Theory and derivation}
\label{sec:ri-theory}

\subsection{Density fitting as a least-squares problem}

For each orbital product
$\rho_{pq}(\mathbf r)=\phi_p(\mathbf r)\phi_q(\mathbf r)$, introduce an
auxiliary expansion
\begin{equation}
  \tilde\rho_{pq}(\mathbf r)
  = \sum_{P} c_P^{pq}\,\chi_P(\mathbf r),
  \label{eq:ri-fit}
\end{equation}
where $\{\chi_P\}$ is the auxiliary basis.  The coefficients are chosen
by minimizing the Coulomb self-repulsion of the residual,
\begin{equation}
  J[\epsilon]
  = \frac{1}{2}\iint
    \epsilon(\mathbf r)\,\frac{1}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\,
    \epsilon(\mathbf r')\,d\mathbf r\,d\mathbf r',
  \qquad
  \epsilon = \tilde\rho_{pq}-\rho_{pq}.
  \label{eq:ri-metric}
\end{equation}
Stationarity with respect to $c_P^{pq}$ gives the linear system
\begin{align}
  \sum_{Q} S_{PQ}\,c_Q^{pq} &= b_{pq,P}, \nonumber\\
  S_{PQ} &= (P|Q), \qquad b_{pq,P}=(pq|P),
  \label{eq:ri-normal}
\end{align}
so
\begin{equation}
  c^{pq}=S^{-1}b^{pq}.
  \label{eq:ri-coeff}
\end{equation}
Approximating one product in the integral by its fit and keeping the
other exact gives
\begin{equation}
  (pq|rs)
  \approx \left(\tilde\rho_{pq}\big|\rho_{rs}\right)
  = \left(c^{pq}\right)^{T} S\,c^{rs}
  = b_{pq}^{T} S^{-1} b_{rs}
  = \sum_{PQ}(pq|P)\,S^{-1}_{PQ}\,(Q|rs),
  \label{eq:ri}
\end{equation}
which is the RI approximation used by the code.  The same metric is
used for both products; this symmetrizes the approximation and is the
standard density-fitting form.  The residual metric
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ri-metric} is the origin of the physical error: the fit
is optimal in the Coulomb norm, not in the wavefunction energy.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$B S^{-1/2}$}{B S^-1/2} factorization}

Because $S$ is symmetric and positive definite on its retained
subspace, define the symmetric inverse square root
\begin{equation}
  S = U\,\Lambda\,U^{T},
  \qquad
  J \equiv S^{-1/2}
  = U\,\Lambda^{-1/2}\,U^{T},
  \qquad
  J^{T}=J.
  \label{eq:ri-j}
\end{equation}
With $W=BJ$, the four-center tensor is
\begin{equation}
  \left(W W^{T}\right)_{pq,rs}
  = \sum_{P} W_{pqP}W_{rsP}
  = \left(B S^{-1}B^{T}\right)_{pq,rs}
  = (pq|rs)_{\mathrm{RI}}.
  \label{eq:ri-assembly}
\end{equation}
The implementation builds $W$ as a matrix product and then one GEMM
over the pair index.  The two-step form is the implementation, not an
optimization: an algebraically equivalent single four-operand einsum
showed absolute errors of order $10^{-6}$ on a $96^4$ array on this
stack, and two such chains differed by eight percent in the rotation
gradient.

Directions with eigenvalue below \texttt{rcond}~$\times$~the largest
eigenvalue are truncated to zero in $J$; the default \texttt{rcond} is
$10^{-12}$, and the number of dropped directions is reported by the
factor container.  Truncation is a numerical regularization of the
metric inverse, not part of the physical RI approximation.

\subsection{Derivative of the symmetric inverse square root}

Let $\dot S$ be the derivative of the metric along a nuclear
displacement, and let $\dot J$ be the corresponding derivative of
$J=S^{-1/2}$.  From the eigendecomposition and the Daleckii--Krein
divided difference,
\begin{equation}
  \dot J
  = U\left[
      f^{[1]}(\lambda_i,\lambda_j)\circ
      \left(U^{T}\dot S\,U\right)
    \right]U^{T},
  \qquad
  f(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1/2},
  \label{eq:ri-dj}
\end{equation}
where $\circ$ is the Hadamard product and
\begin{equation}
  f^{[1]}(x,y)=
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{f(x)-f(y)}{x-y}
      = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}\sqrt{y}\,(\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y})},
      & x\neq y,\\[2mm]
    f'(x)=-\dfrac{1}{2}x^{-3/2}, & x=y.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:ri-divided}
\end{equation}
The diagonal limit is finite at an exact degeneracy, which is why the
differentiated path does not eigendecompose inside JAX and does not
return NaN when two metric eigenvalues coincide.  The same rule is
exposed as \texttt{inv\_sqrt\_metric\_jvp}.  The derivative path uses
the factor-level form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ri-assembly}: the host code
supplies $B$, $J$, and their nuclear derivatives, and a pure-JAX
function with an explicit \texttt{custom\_jvp} maps $(B,J)$ to the
four-center tensor.  No four-center derivative integral is ever formed.

\subsection{Error sources}

Three error classes have different origins and different remedies.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Implementation error.}  The two-step assembly against an
  independent DF reconstruction is $5.8\times10^{-13}$ on
  H$_2$O/6-31G and $1.3\times10^{-12}$ on N$_2$/cc-pVDZ.  This is the
  numerical floor of the contraction, not a physical error.
\item \emph{Fitting error.}  The residual of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:ri-metric} propagates into the energy.  At fixed
  orbitals and CI vector the measured NEVPT2 correction difference is
  $+1.1\times10^{-5}$~Ha on N$_2$/cc-pVDZ and $-7.2\times10^{-6}$~Ha on
  CO/cc-pVDZ; on ThO/6-31G with the \texttt{autoaux} set it is
  $-1.8\times10^{-6}$~Ha.  The sign is not fixed, because the fit is
  not variational in the wavefunction.
\item \emph{Derivative-chain error.}  The RI-CASCI gradient against a
  finite difference of the same RI energy is $9.6\times10^{-8}$ on
  H$_2$O/6-31G and $3.1\times10^{-7}$ on N$_2$/cc-pVDZ, absolute, in
  Eh/bohr.
\end{itemize}

The physical error is controlled by the fitting basis, not by the code.
The values sit at the lower edge of the usual RI band
($10^{-5}$--$10^{-3}$~Ha for correlation energies) and must be quoted
with the fitting basis and the system attached.

\subsection{Memory scaling}

Let $n_{\mathrm{ctr}}$ be the number of contracted shell coefficients
whose derivative is materialized by the dense path.  The dense
integral-derivative array carries three Cartesian components and has
\begin{equation}
  N_{\mathrm{dense}} = 3\,n_{\mathrm{ctr}}\,n_{\mathrm{ao}}^{4}
  \label{eq:ri-dense-elements}
\end{equation}
elements, that is $8N_{\mathrm{dense}}$ bytes in double precision.  The
RI three-center factor derivative for one atom, over the same three
Cartesian directions, has
\begin{equation}
  N_{\mathrm{RI}}^{(1)}
  = 3\,n_{\mathrm{ao}}^{2}\,n_{\mathrm{aux}}
  \label{eq:ri-factor-elements}
\end{equation}
elements, plus the two metric-derivative matrices of size
$3n_{\mathrm{aux}}^{2}$.  The ratio of the leading terms is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \frac{N_{\mathrm{dense}}}{N_{\mathrm{RI}}^{(1)}}
  = \frac{3\,n_{\mathrm{ctr}}\,n_{\mathrm{ao}}^{4}}
         {3\,n_{\mathrm{ao}}^{2}\,n_{\mathrm{aux}}}
  = \frac{n_{\mathrm{ctr}}\,n_{\mathrm{ao}}^{2}}
         {n_{\mathrm{aux}}}.
  \label{eq:ri-memory-ratio}
\end{equation}
The ratio grows with $n_{\mathrm{ao}}^{2}$ and falls with $n_{\mathrm{aux}}$;
the auxiliary basis therefore has to grow only linearly with system size
to keep the derivative working set below the dense array.  The table in
Section~\ref{sec:ri-example} compares the dense array with the peak of
the whole RI chain, not with the single per-atom factor: the chain peak
includes several factor arrays and intermediates, so its ratio is
smaller than Eq.~\eqref{eq:ri-memory-ratio}.  The value path still
materializes the dense \texttt{nao}$^4$ tensor, because that is the
object the CI layer consumes; only the derivative path is factor-level.

\texttt{rcond} is relative to the largest metric eigenvalue and
defaults to $10^{-12}$.  \texttt{auxbasis=None} means the priority
list; when the caller needs a specific family (for example cc-pVDZ-RI
with a cc-pVDZ orbital basis), pass it explicitly.
\texttt{ri\_eri\_deriv} materializes the full
\texttt{(3, nao, nao, nao, nao)} derivative and refuses to allocate
above a caller-supplied byte limit; production code uses the factor
derivatives with \texttt{ri\_eri\_deriv\_contract} or the JAX rule
instead.

The intended nuclear-gradient recipe is

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
from diffmrc.core import ri

factors = ri.ri_factors(mol, auxmol)              # host, numpy
E = energy_from_factors(factors.b, factors.jhalf) # pure JAX chain
dE_db, dE_djhalf = jax.grad(E, argnums=(0, 1))(
    factors.b, factors.jhalf)
for atom in range(mol.natm):
    d = ri.ri_factors_deriv(mol, auxmol, atom, factors)
    dE_dR[atom] = (np.sum(dE_db * d.db)
                   + np.sum(dE_djhalf * d.djhalf))
\end{lstlisting}

The only derivative integrals touched are the three-center
\texttt{ip1}/\texttt{ip2} arrays and the metric \texttt{ip1}/\texttt{ip2}
arrays.

\subsection{Axis and sign conventions}
\label{sec:ri-conventions}

The conventions are fixed in the module and verified by isolated finite
differences; they are repeated here because the \texttt{libcint} names
are misleading.

\begin{itemize}
\item $B[\mu,\nu,P] = (\mu\nu|P)$: both orbital indices first, the
  auxiliary index last.  $S[P,Q] = (P|Q)$.
\item $\texttt{eri\_ao}[\mu,\nu,\lambda,\sigma] =
  (\mu\nu|\lambda\sigma)$ in chemist notation.
\item \texttt{int3c2e\_ip1} differentiates the \emph{first} orbital
  center and \texttt{int3c2e\_ip2} differentiates the \emph{auxiliary}
  center, not the second orbital center.  \texttt{int3c2e\_ip3} is not
  exported by \texttt{libcint} on this stack.
\item The \texttt{ip} series carries the electronic-coordinate sign:
  $\partial(\mu\nu|P)/\partial R_{c(\mu)} = -\texttt{ip1}$.  The second
  orbital center is $-\texttt{ip1}$ with the orbital-pair axes
  exchanged, and the auxiliary center is $-\texttt{ip2}$.
\item For the metric,
  $dS/dR_A = -(\texttt{ip1}[P\in A] + \texttt{ip2}[Q\in A])$.
\end{itemize}

The isolated checks displace the molecule with the auxiliary basis fixed
and the auxiliary basis with the molecule fixed: the two groups of terms
reproduce their finite differences to $7.9\times10^{-11}$, and the sum
reproduces the full displacement to $4.8\times10^{-11}$.

\subsection{A standalone gradient script}

The core of \texttt{examples/ri\_df.py} is

from diffmrc.core import ri
from diffmrc.core.casci import cs_casci

            basis='6-31g', unit='Angstrom', verbose=0)

eri_ri = ri.ri_eri_ao(mol, auxmol, factors=factors)

e_ri, ci_ri = cs_casci(h1_mo[:6, :6], eri_mo_ri[:6, :6, :6, :6],
                       ncore=4, nact=2, nele_act=2, return_ci=True)

for atom in range(mol.natm):
    d3 = ri.ri_three_center_deriv(mol, auxmol, atom)
    # compare d3 against a central difference of ri_three_center
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Pitfalls}
\label{sec:ri-pitfalls}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The SCF layer remains dense.}  RI removes the memory wall
  of the correlation stage, not of the reference determinant.  A
  full-chain nuclear gradient for an actinide still requires an RI-JK
  SCF or an explicit frozen-reference partial-derivative semantics.
\item \emph{Second derivatives are not implemented.}  The factor-level
  \texttt{custom\_jvp} is first order.  Differentiating the reverse
  pass a second time is possible in principle but is outside the
  validated surface; the analytic $S^{-1/2}$ rule is available
  first-order only.
\item \emph{Actinide fitting sets are missing.}  The def2 and
  cc-pVDZ-RI fitting families stop at Rn.  For Th the module falls back
  to the orbital basis (\texttt{naux}~$=646$, metric condition number
  $1.5\times10^{9}$), which shifts a CASCI energy by $-0.594$~mHa.  The
  even-tempered construction \texttt{aug\_etb\_for\_dfbasis} is the best
  available substitute ($+0.021$~mHa, \texttt{naux}~$=625$); a
  dedicated actinide JKfit basis remains an open item.
\item \emph{Basis-family matching is the caller's responsibility.}
  \texttt{make\_auxmol} implements a priority list, not a family-aware
  selector.  A cc-pVDZ orbital basis should be paired with cc-pVDZ-RI
  explicitly.
\item \emph{The truncated metric has a floor.}  \texttt{rcond} defaults
  to $10^{-12}$ and no direction was dropped in the systems measured
  here, but the Th fallback metric is already at the $10^{9}$ condition
  number where the truncation choice starts to matter.
\item \emph{The two-step assembly is not cosmetic.}  A one-shot
  four-operand einsum is algebraically equivalent but numerically
  weaker on this stack; the measured rotation-gradient difference was
  eight percent on a degenerate system.
\item \emph{Geometry caches.}  Build a fresh auxiliary molecule and
  factor set per geometry.  The factor cache is keyed on the built
  molecule; an in-place geometry edit is not reliably refreshed.
\end{itemize}

The density-fitting approximation and its error behaviour are
documented in J.~L.~Whitten, \emph{J. Chem. Phys.} \textbf{58}, 4496
(1973); B.~I.~Dunlap, D.~W.~Connolly and J.~R.~Sabin,
\emph{J. Chem. Phys.} \textbf{71}, 3396 (1979);
O.~Vahtras, J.~Alml{\"o}f and M.~W.~Feyereisen,
Chem. Phys. Lett. \textbf{213}, 514 (1993); K.~Eichkorn,

Chem. Phys. Lett. \textbf{240}, 283 (1995); and F.~Weigend,
\emph{Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys.} \textbf{8}, 1057 (2006).  The
eigendecomposition derivative uses the Daleckii--Krein divided
