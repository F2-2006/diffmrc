\chapter{Protocols and options}
\label{ch:protocols}

The package reports convergence and protocol evidence; it does not
enforce it.  This chapter collects the settings that decide whether a
number is trustworthy, with the values the benchmark records were
produced under.  Where a default can be changed by a caller, the
change belongs next to the number it produced, because several of the
gates below are reachable at more than one tolerance and the results
differ at the margins.

\section{Orbital-point convergence}

The two-stage protocol separates two different failure modes: the
optimizer that can no longer lower the energy, and a point that is not
stationary.  The stages and their defaults:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Setting & Value & Meaning \\
\midrule
\texttt{optimize\_tol} & $10^{-6}$ & L-BFGS-B target; loose on purpose \\
\texttt{refine\_target} & $10^{-11}$ & the Newton target, and the verdict \\
\texttt{max\_steps} & $2000$ & L-BFGS-B iteration cap \\
\texttt{refine\_max\_iter} & $80$ & damped-Newton iteration cap \\
\texttt{\_CG\_tol} / \texttt{\_CG\_maxiter} & $10^{-6}$ / $40$ & Newton--CG budget for the step solve \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The verdict is the gradient norm at the point the chain stopped at,
classified by the stop reason:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\texttt{StopReason} & Reading \\
\midrule
\texttt{CONVERGED} & gradient below the refine target \\
\texttt{STALLED\_GRADIENT} & a step no longer reduced the gradient \\
\texttt{GUARD\_STOPPED} & no damped step lowered the energy \\
\texttt{ITERATION\_CAP} & budget exhausted; re-check before claiming convergence \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two details of the Newton stage are load-bearing and easy to lose when
reimplementing.  The Hessian enters only through Hessian-vector
products --- the explicit \texttt{jax.hessian} broadcasts every
intermediate over $n_\mathrm{par}^2$ tangent pairs and was killed by a
402~GB allocation at $n_\mathrm{par} \approx 500$ --- and the
downhill acceptance test is not optional: an unguarded refinement
moved an energy from $-76.07$ to $+1.04$~Ha in this project's history
and printed no error.

\section{State averaging}

The state-averaged factories take \texttt{nroots} and
\texttt{state\_weights}; the weights are normalized and must have one
entry per root.  The measured effect of between-group equal weights
(the second pass) on the reported excitation energies stayed within a
few tenths of an eV for the benchmark systems, while the second pass
usually tightens the final orbital gradient by orders of magnitude.
The benchmark records quote which pass each number came from.

Two root-table facts belong with every state-averaged number.  The
eigensolver returns roots, so a triplet travels as three degenerate
roots; and a per-component NEVPT2 correction can be undefined
(\texttt{nan}) for a pure $|M| = 1$ component, in which case the
group's representative is the lowest component with a finite value.
\texttt{sa\_nevpt2\_state\_table} performs both bookkeeping and
returns the evidence.

\section{Active-space windows}

The frontier-window rules are stated in the Tutorial (its chapter on
choosing an active space).  The part that belongs to a protocol table
is the bookkeeping: the electron count follows the window,

\begin{equation}
  n_\mathrm{ele} = N_\mathrm{ele} - 2\, n_\mathrm{core},
\end{equation}

and a window that widens to three orbitals for the linear two-pi and
amide patterns therefore carries four active electrons, not two.  A
hard-coded two here produces a wrong ground state that the energy
alone will not flag.

\section{The e2\_scale switch}

Both NEVPT2 factories accept \texttt{e2\_scale}.  With
\texttt{e2\_scale = 0} the perturbation correction is switched off and
the call reduces to the CASSCF energy of the same chain --- the
identity used by the test suite to pin the joint and state-averaged
factories against the validated single-root path.  With the default
value of $1.0$ the correction is included.  There is no other
intermediate value with a physical meaning; the switch exists for
verification.

\section{Differentiation settings}

The theta-only factories are safe inside \texttt{jax.jit}; the
\texttt{Mole}-based ones are not, and the failure mode is a tracing
error at compile time rather than a wrong number.  The second-order
paths (the refinement, the trust-region polish) differentiate the
gradient with forward-mode \texttt{jvp} calls; a caller wrapping the
whole convergence in \texttt{jit} should expect the compile of those
nodes to dominate the first evaluation.

\section{MC-PDFT on-top functionals (tPBE, tPBE0)}
\label{sec:protocol-mcpdft}

The reported energy of \texttt{--method mcpdft} is

\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{MC\text{-}PDFT}} \;=\; E_{\mathrm{mcwfn}} \;+\; E_{ot},
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{mcwfn}} \;=\; V_{nn} + \langle h | D \rangle
+ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle J[D] \,|\, D \rangle ,
\end{equation}

with $D$ the \emph{full} (core plus active) one-body density and
$E_{ot}$ the on-top functional of the translated spin densities,
evaluated on a frozen quadrature grid.  The conventions that the
validation had to pin down, and that a reimplementation must
reproduce, live in \texttt{core.mcpdft}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the translation uses gradient rows
    $\rho'_{\sigma} = (\nabla\rho/2)(1 \pm \zeta)$ with \textbf{no}
    $\nabla\zeta$ term (Li Manni et al.\ 2014; the engine's
    \texttt{get\_rho\_translated});
  \item the on-top pair density is the convention-free factorized form
    $\Pi = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum \phi^4 (A - B_{ss})
    + (\rho_f\rho_f - \rho_a\rho_a)$, with $A$ the M-block product
    identical to the stack's spin-summed 2-RDM mechanism;
  \item the xc kernel is the package's own closed-form PBE
    (\texttt{core.mcpdft.pbe\_exc}), because the pyscfad/libxc wrapper
    cannot be traced under \texttt{jit}; it matches libxc to
    $2\times10^{-15}$ in the physical domain and deviates only below
    $\rho \sim 10^{-13}$ (libxc's internal guards), where the grid
    weights put the integral contribution below $10^{-25}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The frozen grid.}
The quadrature is built once and shared by every evaluation, so
geometry derivatives omit the grid-velocity term.  The calibration on
CO/6-31G bounds that term at $7.3\times10^{-5}$~Eh/bohr at grid
level~3, falling to $2.5\times10^{-5}$ at level~4 -- three orders of
magnitude below the wavefunction response it rides on.

\paragraph{One-step protocol and the relaxed variant.}
The orbitals are the CASSCF-driven ones and the on-top energy is
evaluated once; this is the DVS paper's protocol.  The orbital point
is \emph{not} stationary for the on-top functional (measured
$|\nabla E| \approx 3\times10^{-3}$ there).  The theta chain can also
minimize the on-top energy itself, which converges to a fully relaxed
stationary point below the one-step value by
$3.0\times10^{-4}$~Eh on H$_2$/6-31G; the two numbers answer different
questions and the reports say which one they carry.

\paragraph{The tPBE0 hybrid.}
\texttt{--method mcpdft0} is the 0.25 hybrid.  Its engine
decomposition is $E = E_{\mathrm{classical}} + \lambda (E_x + E_c)
+ (1-\lambda) E_{ot}$ with $\lambda = 0.25$, $E_x$ the HF-like
exchange of the full density and $E_c$ the active-space cumulant
contraction.  The standard RDM split gives $E_x + E_c =
E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}} - E_{\mathrm{classical}}$, so at the one-step
orbitals the hybrid is the convex combination
$E_{\mathrm{tPBE0}} = 0.75\,E_{\mathrm{tPBE}} + 0.25\,E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}$;
the chain implements exactly this, with no 2-RDM convention in the
path.

\paragraph{Verification anchors.}
Against \texttt{pyscf.mcpdft}: H$_2$/STO-3G reproduces
$e_{tot}/e_{ot}/e_{mcwfn}$ to $10^{-16}$ (golden
$-1.156628479816$; the tPBE0 value $-1.151792318484$ to
$9\times10^{-16}$), H$_2$O/STO-3G to $1.6\times10^{-9}$, and
H$_2$/6-31G to $1.3\times10^{-8}$ -- the engine's own FCI convergence
floor, measured as its Davidson vector differing from a full block
decomposition by $\sim10^{-7}$.  The rotation-chart AD gradient agrees
with finite differences to $10^{-10}$, and the fully relaxed
optimization reaches $|\nabla| = 1.4\times10^{-14}$.

\paragraph{The (lambda, N) DVS scan.}
\texttt{driver.dvs.dvs\_scan} builds the candidate orbital sets of the
DVS construction ($G(\lambda) = F - \lambda K$ diagonalization, the
exact t369 reproduction), picks windows with the APC chooser (pyscf's
\texttt{mcscf.apc}, the authors' implementation), and carries each
candidate through the one-step columns; the selection is the lowest
mean on-top energy with the runner-up gap reported.  Windows whose
determinant count exceeds \texttt{max\_ndet} (default 2000) are
refused with the count recorded: the in-stack CI is a full determinant
decomposition, so the paper's ZnH/CuCl $(12,12)/(14,14)$ windows stay
engine-side by design.
