\chapter{The differentiable chain}
\label{ch:differentiable-chain}

\section{Purpose and scope}

This chapter describes how the package makes a multireference energy
differentiable and records the design facts that shaped the
implementation.  The chain covers CASCI, CASSCF, state-averaged CASSCF,
and the NEVPT2 correction.  It applies to molecular systems with a
fixed active-space partition and a full-electron or effective-core
Hamiltonian whose one- and two-electron integrals are differentiable in
the nuclear coordinates.

The scope excludes three cases.  A non-variational correction whose
orbitals are optimized on a different surface needs the response term
of Section~\ref{sec:chain-zvector}; the frozen derivative alone is not
the physical derivative.  A system whose orbital Hessian has a
near-null direction needs the pseudo-inverse construction of
Chapter~\ref{ch:second-derivatives}; a plain solve amplifies the null
component.  A wavefunction whose CI space is enlarged by a
non-differentiable selection rule is outside the contract: every
quantity differentiated here is a smooth function of a fixed parameter
vector.

\section{Theory and derivation}
\label{sec:chain-theory}

\subsection{Exponential parameterization}

Let $C_0$ be a reference coefficient matrix of shape
$n_{\mathrm{ao}}\times n_{\mathrm{mo}}$, and let
$\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\mathrm{par}}}$ be a vector of rotation
parameters.  The parameterized orbitals are
\begin{equation}
  C(\theta) = C_0\,R(\theta),
  \qquad
  R(\theta) = \prod_{k=1}^{n_{\mathrm{par}}} \exp\!\left(\theta_k A_k\right),
  \label{eq:chain-exp}
\end{equation}
where the product is ordered and
\begin{equation}
  A_k = e_{i_k}e_{j_k}^{T} - e_{j_k}e_{i_k}^{T},
  \qquad
  A_k^{T} = -A_k,
  \qquad
  A_k^{2} = -\left(e_{i_k}e_{i_k}^{T} + e_{j_k}e_{j_k}^{T}\right)
  \label{eq:chain-generator}
\end{equation}
is the elementary antisymmetric generator of the pair
$(i_k,j_k)$.  Each factor is a plane rotation,
\begin{equation}
  \exp(\theta_k A_k)
  = \cos\theta_k\left(e_{i_k}e_{i_k}^{T}+e_{j_k}e_{j_k}^{T}\right)
  + \sin\theta_k\left(e_{j_k}e_{i_k}^{T}-e_{i_k}e_{j_k}^{T}\right)
  + \sum_{m\notin\{i_k,j_k\}} e_m e_m^{T},
  \label{eq:chain-givens}
\end{equation}
so $R(\theta)$ is orthogonal, $R^{T}R=I$, and the transformed
coefficients remain orthonormal when $C_0^{T}S C_0=I$ in the overlap
metric.

The factors do not commute for different pairs, so the ordered product
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain-exp} is not the single exponential
$\exp(\sum_k\theta_k A_k)$ in general.  The implementation evaluates
the ordered product directly as a sequence of Givens updates
(\texttt{rotation.block\_rotation}); the distinction matters when a
closed-form derivative is written down, because the derivative of the
product is not the product of the derivatives in the same order.

The allowed pair set is
\begin{equation}
  (i,a),\quad (i,v),\quad (a,v),
  \qquad i\in\mathrm{core},\ a\in\mathrm{active},\ v\in\mathrm{virtual}.
  \label{eq:chain-pairs}
\end{equation}
Intra-block rotations (core--core, active--active, virtual--virtual)
leave the CASSCF energy invariant: the core block is fully occupied,
the virtual block is empty, and the active block is complete inside the
CI step.  They are therefore omitted.  The core--virtual family is not
redundant.  On N$_2$/6-31G CAS(2,2) the measured energies are
$-108.903578776806$~Eh with 32 parameters (core--active and
active--virtual only), $-108.903650545967$~Eh with 92 parameters
(adding core--virtual), and the same value with 107 parameters (adding
intra-core).  The 0.045~kcal/mol defect of the pruned set is systematic
and does not show up as a convergence failure.

\subsection{Energy derivative}

Let the energy depend on the orbitals through a differentiable
functional $E(C)$, and let $G=\partial E/\partial C$ denote the
$n_{\mathrm{ao}}\times n_{\mathrm{mo}}$ matrix of partial
derivatives.  The chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial E}{\partial\theta_k}
  = \mathrm{tr}\!\left[
      G^{T} C_0 \frac{\partial R}{\partial\theta_k}
    \right],
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial R}{\partial\theta_k}
  = \left(\prod_{m<k}\exp(\theta_m A_m)\right)
    A_k \exp(\theta_k A_k)
    \left(\prod_{m>k}\exp(\theta_m A_m)\right).
  \label{eq:chain-theta-derivative}
\end{equation}
The implementation never forms Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain-theta-derivative}
by hand; JAX differentiates the ordered Givens product and the energy
composition.  The equivalence with the MO-basis form is useful for
diagnostics.  For a Givens rotation of the pair $(i,j)$ the orbital
gradient is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial E}{\partial\theta_{ij}}
  = 2\left(F_{ji}-F_{ij}\right),
  \label{eq:chain-orbital-gradient}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the generalized Fock matrix in the MO basis,
\begin{equation}
  F_{pq} = \sum_r \gamma_{pr}h_{qr}
         + \sum_{rst}\Gamma_{prst}(qr|st),
  \label{eq:chain-generalized-fock}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma$ and $\Gamma$ the state 1- and 2-RDMs.  The factor 2 and
the antisymmetric index order follow from $A_k^{T}=-A_k$; a symmetric
$F$ gives zero, which is the statement that a rotation that does not
change the state has no gradient.

\subsection{The envelope theorem}

Write the relaxed energy as a function of the geometry alone,
\begin{equation}
  E^{*}(R) = E\bigl(\theta^{*}(R),R\bigr),
  \qquad
  \theta^{*}(R) = \arg\min_{\theta} E(\theta,R),
  \label{eq:chain-envelope-def}
\end{equation}
and assume that $\theta^{*}(R)$ is an interior, isolated stationary
point and that $E$ is twice continuously differentiable in a
neighborhood.  Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain-envelope-def} gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dE^{*}}{dR}
  = \left.\frac{\partial E}{\partial R}\right|_{\theta}
  + \left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial\theta}\right)
    \frac{d\theta^{*}}{dR}.
  \label{eq:chain-envelope-raw}
\end{equation}
The stationarity condition
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial E}{\partial\theta}\bigl(\theta^{*}(R),R\bigr)=0
  \label{eq:chain-stationarity}
\end{equation}
annihilates the second term, so
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dE^{*}}{dR}
  = \left.\frac{\partial E}{\partial R}\right|_{\theta^{*}}.
  \label{eq:chain-envelope}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:chain-envelope} is the envelope theorem used by the
production gradient: the orbital parameters are frozen at their
stationary values and the reference orbitals are recomputed at the
displaced geometry.  Both ingredients are required.  Holding $C_0$
fixed at the reference geometry is a different physical orbital set and
produces a spurious gradient of order $4\times10^{-1}$~Eh/bohr; taking
$C_0$ from a converged CASSCF calculation at the reference geometry is
also not the displaced reference and produces an error of order
$4\times10^{-3}$~Eh/bohr.

Three conditions are implicit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain-envelope}:

\begin{itemize}
\item the stationary point is interior, so no active constraint enters
  the derivative;
\item the branch is fixed, so $\theta^{*}(R)$ is a differentiable
  function rather than a set-valued map at a bifurcation;
\item the reference orbitals are a differentiable function of the
  geometry, including any gauge choice used to align degenerate blocks.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Non-variational corrections and the Z-vector equation}
\label{sec:chain-zvector}

NEVPT2 is not variational in the orbitals.  Let
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{tot}}(\theta,R)
  = E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}(\theta,R) + e_2(\theta,R),
  \label{eq:chain-total-energy}
\end{equation}
and let $\theta^{*}$ minimize $E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}$, not
$E_{\mathrm{tot}}$.  Differentiating along the physical path,
\begin{align}
  \frac{dE_{\mathrm{tot}}}{dR}
  &= \left.\frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}}{\partial R}\right|_{\theta}
   + \left.\frac{\partial e_2}{\partial R}\right|_{\theta}
   + \left(\frac{\partial e_2}{\partial\theta}\right)
     \frac{d\theta^{*}}{dR}, \nonumber\\
  &= g_{\mathrm{frozen}} + s^{T}\frac{d\theta^{*}}{dR},
  \label{eq:chain-nonvar-raw}
\end{align}
where the second line defines the frozen gradient and the correlation
source.
because the CASSCF stationarity condition removes
$(\partial E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}/\partial\theta)$ but not
$(\partial e_2/\partial\theta)$.  The orbital response follows from
differentiating the stationarity condition:
\begin{equation}
  H_{\theta\theta}\frac{d\theta^{*}}{dR} + M = 0,
  \qquad
  H_{\theta\theta}
  = \frac{\partial^{2}E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}}{\partial\theta^{2}},
  \qquad
  M = \frac{\partial^{2}E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}}{\partial\theta\,\partial R}.
  \label{eq:chain-response-eq}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\theta^{*}}{dR} = -H_{\theta\theta}^{-1}M,
  \label{eq:chain-dtheta}
\end{equation}
and the relaxed gradient is
\begin{equation}
  g_{\mathrm{relaxed}}
  = g_{\mathrm{frozen}} - M^{T}H_{\theta\theta}^{-1}s,
  \qquad
  s = \frac{\partial e_2}{\partial\theta},
  \label{eq:chain-zvector}
\end{equation}
with the frozen gradient
$g_{\mathrm{frozen}}=\partial_R(E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}+e_2)|_{\theta^{*}}$.
The vector
\begin{equation}
  z = H_{\theta\theta}^{-1}s
  \label{eq:chain-z}
\end{equation}
is the orbital Z-vector.  One linear solve serves every Cartesian
direction, which is the adjoint form of the response; the direct form
would solve one linear system per displacement.

The CI coordinates need no separate multiplier in this reduced space.
Both energy functions solve the CI eigenvalue problem internally, so
their $\theta$ and $R$ derivatives are taken along the CI-relaxed
manifold.  The CI block of the full Hessian is replaced by its Schur
complement, which is the $H_{\theta\theta}$ above.  Working in the full
$(\theta,c)$ space would add a CI multiplier, but that multiplier is
annihilated by the variational CI condition for $E_{\mathrm{CASSCF}}$
and only reshuffles the same reduced Hessian.

The Hessian in Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain-zvector} is singular along
directions that leave the CASSCF energy invariant.  The implementation
uses a truncated pseudo-inverse with a relative cutoff, not a direct
solve; the construction and the null-space argument are in
Chapter~\ref{ch:second-derivatives}.  On H$_2$O/6-31G the CASSCF
Hessian carries one soft mode of eigenvalue $3.8\times10^{-6}$ and
condition number $2.3\times10^{7}$ whose response is not part of the
correlation source; the relaxed-gradient record uses
\texttt{rcond}$=10^{-7}$ to cut it.

\section{Input and operation}
\label{sec:chain-input}

The chain has two factory families.  The \emph{joint} factories
(\texttt{driver.nevpt2.make\_nevpt2\_joint\_fn} and its state-averaged
sibling) take the molecule and $\theta$ and rebuild the reference SCF
inside the call; they are the geometry-differentiable path.  The
\emph{theta-only} factories
(\texttt{make\_nevpt2\_theta\_fn}, \texttt{make\_nevpt2\_sa\_theta\_fn})
compute the SCF and the integrals once at construction and return a
callable of $\theta$ alone.  The theta-only form exists because this
pyscfad build cannot trace its SCF inside \texttt{jax.jit}, and the
orbital optimizer wraps its objective in \texttt{jit}.

A minimal CASSCF gradient skeleton is
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
import jax
jax.config.update('jax_enable_x64', True)
from jax import numpy as jnp
from pyscfad import gto as pgto, scf as pscf

from diffmrc.core.rotation import block_rotation
from diffmrc.core.orblib import orbital_pairs
from diffmrc.core.optimizer import optimize_theta
from diffmrc.core.grad import (make_energy_fn, make_grad_fn,
                               solve_theta_star, nuclear_gradient)

mol = pgto.Mole()
mol.atom = 'O 0 0 0; H 0.7557 0 0.5880; H -0.7557 0 0.5880'
mol.basis = '6-31g'
mol.build()
nmo, ncore, nact, nele = mol.nao_nr(), 4, 2, 2
pairs = orbital_pairs(ncore, nact, nmo, include_cv=True)
npar = len(pairs)

mf = pscf.RHF(mol)
mf.kernel()
C_rhf = jnp.asarray(mf.mo_coeff)
h1e = mol.intor('int1e_kin') + mol.intor('int1e_nuc')
eri = mol.intor('int2e', aosym='s1')
E_cas = make_energy_fn(ncore, nact, nele, use_blocked=True)

E_theta = lambda t: E_cas(C_rhf @ block_rotation(t, nmo, pairs),
                          h1e, eri, mol.energy_nuc())
theta, gnorm = solve_theta_star(mol, C_rhf, E_theta, npar,
                                optimize_theta, steps=2000,
                                g_conv=1e-12)
g = nuclear_gradient(mol, make_grad_fn(nmo, pairs, theta, E_cas, pscf))
\end{lstlisting}

The parameter list \texttt{pairs} is the contract: it must include
core--active, core--virtual, and active--virtual pairs.
\texttt{ncore}, \texttt{nact}, and \texttt{nele} are spatial-orbital
and electron counts.  \texttt{theta} is in radians and is defined
relative to the reference coefficient matrix; \texttt{gnorm} is the
Euclidean norm of $\partial E/\partial\theta$ at the returned point.
A value above $10^{-6}$ means the point is not usable as a stationary
reference.  The gradient is returned in Eh/bohr with shape
\texttt{(natm, 3)}.

For a non-variational correction the caller builds the two energy
functions and calls the Z-vector wrapper, as in
Chapter~\ref{ch:new-modules}:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
from diffmrc.driver.nevpt2_zvector import (
    make_nevpt2_joint_fn_si_exact, nevpt2_relaxed_gradient_zvector)

def scf_builder(m):
    mf = pscf.RHF(m)
    mf.conv_tol = 1e-13
    mf.conv_tol_grad = 1e-10
    return mf

E0 = make_nevpt2_joint_fn_si_exact(ncore, nact, nele, nmo, pairs,
                                   scf_builder, e2_scale=0.0,
                                   align_ref=C_rhf,
                                   align_bounds=[(0, ncore),
                                                 (ncore, ncore + nact),
                                                 (ncore + nact, nmo)])
E2 = make_nevpt2_joint_fn_si_exact(ncore, nact, nele, nmo, pairs,
                                   scf_builder, e2_scale=1.0,
                                   align_ref=C_rhf,
                                   align_bounds=[(0, ncore),
                                                 (ncore, ncore + nact),
                                                 (ncore + nact, nmo)])
g_relaxed, info = nevpt2_relaxed_gradient_zvector(
    mol, theta, E0, E2, rcond=1e-7)
\end{lstlisting}
\texttt{theta} must be the stationary point of the same aligned CASSCF
surface that \texttt{E0} evaluates; a parameter vector optimized against
a different reference gauge is not the response point, even if the
energy is close.

\section{Worked example}
\label{sec:chain-example}

\subsection{H\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}/STO-3G: the zero-correlation anchor}

The first example isolates the variational part of the chain.  On
H$_2$/STO-3G at $R=0.74$~\AA{} the CAS(2,2) energy is
$-1.137283834489$~Eh (\texttt{tests/core/test\_iterative\_ci.py}).  The
joint NEVPT2 factory at \texttt{e2\_scale=0.0} returns the same CASSCF
energy: the test bound is $10^{-5}$~Eh
(\texttt{tests/driver/test\_nevpt2.py}).  With the correlation scaled
off, the joint gradient and the independent CASSCF gradient agree to
$10^{-10}$~Eh/bohr, and the gradient is translationally invariant to
$10^{-8}$~Eh/bohr on this system.  The system has two orbitals only, so
the correlation term is identically zero and the example tests the
variational chain, not the perturbation layer.

The numerical output to record is therefore a set of agreement
bounds, not a gradient component: the energy anchor
$-1.137283834489$~Eh, the factory difference below $10^{-5}$~Eh, and
the gradient difference below $10^{-10}$~Eh/bohr.  A finite-difference
pair on this system is not recorded in the release tree; the
finite-difference comparison is the next example.

\subsection{H\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}O/6-31G: AD against the
finite-difference golden standard}

The production regression compares the AD gradient with a central
difference of an independent analytic CASSCF energy at $h=10^{-3}$~bohr
(\texttt{experiments/gradient\_regression.py}).  On H$_2$O/6-31G
CAS(2,2) the recorded largest deviation over converged points is
$3.5\times10^{-8}$~Eh/bohr (the baseline in that file is
$3.5\times10^{-8}$; the analytic-reference run in
\texttt{experiments/gradient\_gold\_validation.py} reports
$3.496256317792046\times10^{-8}$).  The archived analytic reference
gradient is, in Eh/bohr,
\begin{lstlisting}
[ 1.06581410e-10,  7.78044296e-09, -2.32218066e-02,
 -4.76657289e-03,  2.55795385e-10,  1.16109088e-02,
  4.76657296e-03, -1.42108547e-10,  1.16109094e-02]
\end{lstlisting}
The AD gradient reproduces this vector within the stated deviation;
the two near-zero $x$ components are constrained by the molecular
plane and are the first place a broken invariance shows up.

\subsection{N\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}/6-31G: the parameter set is part
of the answer}

The third example is the parameter-set record of
\texttt{core.orblib}.  On N$_2$/6-31G CAS(2,2), against an independent
CASSCF implementation:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Parameter set & Count & $E_{\mathrm{CAS}}$ (Eh) & Deviation (kcal/mol) \\
\midrule
core--active and active--virtual & 32 & $-108.903578776806$ & $0.045036$ \\
adding core--virtual & 92 & $-108.903650545967$ & $8\times10^{-10}$ \\

The last row confirms that intra-core rotations are redundant.  The
middle row shows that the core--virtual family is not: dropping it
leaves the orbital optimization at a non-stationary point whose energy
is too high by 0.045~kcal/mol.

\section{Pitfalls}
\label{sec:chain-pitfalls}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Pruning the pair list.}  Core--virtual rotations are not
  redundant.  The failure is a smooth, systematic energy defect, not a
  convergence failure; it is invisible without an independent
  reference.
\item \emph{Using the raw CASSCF orbitals as the reference.}  The
  reference $C_0$ must be the geometry-dependent SCF solution, not the
  converged CASSCF coefficients from the reference geometry.  The
  measured error is $4\times10^{-3}$~Eh/bohr for the mildest case and
  $4\times10^{-1}$~Eh/bohr when $C_0$ is held fixed.
\item \emph{Calling a joint factory inside \texttt{jit}.}  This
  pyscfad build cannot trace the SCF inside a jit context.  Use the
  theta-only factory for the optimizer and the joint factory for the
  implicit Hessian.
\item \emph{Forgetting the gauge alignment.}  The SCF solution is
  defined up to orthogonal mixing inside degenerate blocks.  An
  unaligned reference makes the same $\theta$ mean different orbitals
  at neighbouring geometries; the measured fixed-theta energy slope on
  H$_2$O/6-31G changes from $-5.8$ to $-0.0528$~Eh/bohr under a phase
  flip.  Pass the base-geometry coefficients as \texttt{align\_ref}
  and the block bounds as \texttt{align\_bounds}.
\item \emph{Applying the envelope theorem to a non-variational
  correction.}  The frozen derivative of NEVPT2 is not the relaxed
  derivative.  The response term of
  Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain-zvector} is required; on H$_2$O it is not small.
\item \emph{Finite differences near a zero derivative.}  A central
  difference of a near-zero mixed derivative is unreliable: this
  project measured errors of over one hundredfold, growing as the step
  shrank.  Use an invariance or an analytic cross-check instead.
\item \emph{Unblocked eigensolvers on degenerate spectra.}  The plain
  eigensolver's second derivative is NaN at a cross-$S_z$ degeneracy;
  the blocked solver is required for second derivatives, and linear
  molecules with bit-exact degenerate pairs need the Lanczos kernel.
\end{itemize}
